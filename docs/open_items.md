# Open items

Decisions taken without the user's input, and known weaknesses deferred to later PRs. Each entry says what is open, the current default, and where it is revisited. Resolved entries move to the end with their resolution.

## Open

### Voxel GMG on high-contrast SIMP fields (PR 11a → PR 13b, PRs 21–23)

At a SIMP field's own $\lambda_1$ ($s_K = \rho^3$, $s_M = \rho$, $\rho_\min = 10^{-3}$) the lowest modes are spurious island and void-region modes. With island deflation and coarse Chebyshev degree 4, PCG to $10^{-8}$ takes 87 iterations on the checked-in field (the roadmap test, ≤ 100), 109–135 on other seeds of the same recipe and 452 on an unfiltered binary field (`benchmarks/results/gmg_study.json`). Every case converges; only speed differs.

- **Default:** ship as is (user, 2026-10-12: "As long as it should work for general problems now it's good").
- **Candidate fix:** operator-dependent transfers. A SciPy prototype with Jacobi-smoothed, phase-aware prolongation plus deflation reached 34 (checked-in field) and 55 (seed 4), but needs coarse stencils wider than 27 points, which algebraic multigrid has.
- **PR 13b's AMG study** (`amg_study.simp`, the fields as unstructured hex8 meshes): AMG is worse than GMG on the filtered fields at θ = 0 (checked-in field at its own λ₁: 789–911 against 87; at the solid's λ₁ 70–75 against 33; seeds 4 / 5: 646–761 / 136–152 against 133–135 / 107–109) and level on the binary field (446–451 against 413–450; `benchmarks/results/amg_study.json`). AMG's near-null space is the global rigid modes and θ = 0 aggregates across void and solid, while GMG deflates the islands. A strength threshold θ = 0.02 brings the checked-in field at its own λ₁ to 49–51, but at the solid's λ₁ its coarse levels turn dense (1804 nonzeros per row) and coarsening stalls at 1152 rows (build plus PCG unfinished after 16 min); it also raises AMG storage 34–56% on the 13a cases. **Decision without user input (PR 13b):** keep θ = 0 and no `theta` keyword (it would expose a setting with a pathological setup).
- **Revisit:** PRs 21–23: modified mass interpolation removes the spurious modes; a per-island near-null space (as GMG's deflation) or a safe strength threshold for AMG; PR 23 re-runs the contrast test on a real design.

### Thin plates and slender beams (PR 11a; solid shells resolved in 18b)

At σ = $\lambda_1$ the GMG iterations grow like $h^{-1}$ on one-element-thick plates (23 / 37 / 71 / 136 from $16^2$ to $128^2$): coarse hex8 layers lock in bending. Default: documented limitation; the roadmap single-layer test uses $32^2\times1$. M4 (PR 16) added the solid shell; its AMG is PR 18a (`amg()`) and the `solve_modes` default since PR 18b (Resolved below). For hex and voxel plates this stays a documented limitation (remedy: convert to `ssh8`).

p-MG (PR 12) has the same cause on flat elements: its hex8 coarse space locks in bending when the element aspect ratio $a \gg 1$. Single-layer hex20 plates $16^2\times1$ take 15 / 21 / 63 iterations for $a$ = 1 / 2 / 4 and stagnate at $a = 10$ (620–720); a box with $h_z = h/a$ takes 14 / 17 / 28 / 76; the hex27 plate $32^2\times1$ at $a = 10$ exceeds 1000. Slenderness with $a = 1$ is fine (beam $20\times2\times2$: 16).

AMG on the hex8 level (PR 13a, `max_coarse` = 100) makes it worse: plates 36 / 85 / 229 / > 1000 for $a$ = 1 / 2 / 4 / 10, boxes 14 / 20 / 52 / 244. The deeper AMG levels lose plate bending (the $a = 1$ plate takes 15 / 19 / 36 with zero / one / two AMG levels); the beam stays at 16. **Decision without user input (PR 13a):** neither AMG on the high-order level nor a bending-aware near-null space is attempted (on hex8; PR 18a adds one for `ssh8`); both are a new setup design rather than a hex8-level AMG. Retargeted to M4 / PR 18b (solid-shell elements, done).

### Anisotropic voxel spacing (PR 11a)

Coarsening is by element count, not spacing: $h_z = h/10$ needs 106 iterations. Default: documented limitation. Spacing-aware semi-coarsening needs line smoothers; no PR owns it yet.

### Binary-field λ₁ miss of the σ pre-estimate (PR 11b)

On an unfiltered binary SIMP field ($32^3$) the GMG-refined pre-estimate lands at 390 λ₁: λ₁ is a void-region mode that 20 iterations from a random start miss (100 find it), and 10-mode LOBPCG does not converge on that field at any σ either. Default: documented limitation (filtered fields and every other tested case are within 1.05 λ₁). PR 13b's SIMP study gives no help: AMG is level with GMG on that field (446–451 against 413–450 PCG iterations). Revisit with PRs 21–23's modified mass interpolation, which removes the void modes.

### Island deflation from phases (PR 11b)

Stiff islands are detected from `s_K` only, so a multi-phase voxel model with $10^3$–$10^5$ material contrast between phases (uniform `s_K`) gets no island deflation. Default: documented. Revisit when a multi-phase contrast case appears (PR 23).

### GMG intermittent extra peak in LOBPCG (PR 11b)

With GMG the LOBPCG process peak is bimodal: in some runs, from about iteration 5, it is about one V-cycle temporary above the step's `memory_analysis` (+12 blocks at $48^3$), in other identical runs not; synchronous dispatch makes it rarer. So `lobpcg_memory` with `gmg_memory` is not a strict bound at mid sizes: 11% low at $64^3$ (fp64 cycle), 4% high at $48^3$ (worst of three runs), 10–12% high at $87^3$; all inside the roadmap's ±20% band. Default: keep the `memory_analysis` envelope `GMG_STEP` and document it. Revisit on the GPU (PR 24: the BFC allocator behaves differently) or if a size falls outside the band. PR 18d's investigation of the plate's bimodal peak ([below](#bimodal-lobpcg-peak-on-the-791k-plate-pr-18c--pr-18d-user-decision-2026-10-19)) may cover this one too (not checked on GMG).

### Run-to-run LOBPCG peak without GMG (found during PR 12, decision without user input)

The chunked 300k-DOF diagonal-pencil peak (`test_estimate_chunks`) varies between identical runs too: 0.95–1.07× `lobpcg_memory` (fp64 basis, 1.24–1.39 against 1.30 GB; fp32 up to 1.04×), and peaks above the estimate, which earlier runs did not show, became the common case on 2026-10-13 with no package change (it fails on `main` as well). Unchunked (30 modes, $m = 36$) it is 1.00–1.16×. Synchronous CPU dispatch makes the excursions smaller but does not remove them, as for GMG. All inside the roadmap's ±20% band. Default, as for GMG: keep the estimator and test against 0.8–1.1× the estimate instead of a strict bound (3% headroom over the worst run; if it trips again and repeated runs show the base level unchanged at about 0.95×, with only the intermittent excursion larger, widen to the roadmap's 1.2×; otherwise investigate the estimator or the step, since an extra fp64 basis copy lands at about 1.17–1.22×). Revisit with the GMG item (PR 24) or if a size falls outside the band. PR 18d: see the plate's [bimodal peak](#bimodal-lobpcg-peak-on-the-791k-plate-pr-18c--pr-18d-user-decision-2026-10-19) (a step-boundary footprint query removes that extra; not checked on this case).

### `splu` ordering for the test-only coarse solve (PR 12, decision without user input)

Geometric nested dissection on the corner coordinates factors 4–8× faster than COLAMD (hex20 $24^3$: 3.5 s / 0.69 GB against 14 s / 1.5 GB), but `Unstructured` carries no coordinates. Default: keep COLAMD instead of adding a `points` argument to a test-only path. `amg` (PR 13a) needs no host direct solve (device Cholesky of a coarsest matrix ≤ 4·`max_coarse`), so this stays as is unless `pmg` leaves the tests.

### `pmg` kept alongside `amg` (PR 13a, decision without user input)

`pmg` (exact host `splu` coarse solve) stays as the test-only reference next to `amg`: its tests and study pin the p-level, and `amg`'s no-AMG-level path is checked against it. Default: keep; remove if it ever becomes a maintenance burden.

### Own SA setup loop instead of `smoothed_aggregation_solver` (PR 13a, decision without user input)

The roadmap says "pyamg smoothed-aggregation setup". `amg` runs its own short loop over pyamg's primitives, because `fit_candidates` keeps zero tentative columns and pyamg's solver hides the resulting zero rows with a pseudo-inverse coarse solve, while the roadmap requires the columns dropped (dense Cholesky). The first-level prolongator equals pyamg's exactly (test). Default: keep.

### Masked near-null space (PR 13a, decision without user input)

$B$ has the rows of constrained corner DOFs zeroed, as the roadmap says. Unmasked $B$ gave the same iteration counts but makes the constrained rows of the all-hex8 $P_0$ nonzero (0.19 on hex8 $8^3$ with sliding faces), so the fine mask would no longer be the only mask needed. Default: masked.

### AMG memory constants calibrated on box meshes (PR 13b, decision without user input)

`amg_memory`'s per-corner-DOF storage constant, its step constants `AMG_STEP` and its setup term are fitted on box meshes (hex20 $8^3$–$44^3$, hex27 $6^3$–$24^3$, hex8 $12^3$–$68^3$; a distorted hex20 box stays inside the bound). Other geometries aggregate differently. Default: document; revisit if a real mesh falls outside the roadmap's ±20% band.

### AMG build peak above 1M DOF (PR 13b, decision without user input)

The measured setup peak per corner DOF grows about 0.65 kB per decade of $n_c$ (hex20 3.7 / 4.3 / 4.8 kB at $12^3$ / $24^3$ / $44^3$); `AMG_BUILD` extrapolates that trend to 10M DOF (14.6 GB hex20, 46.6 GB hex8). hex27 has two points ($12^3$, $24^3$) that grow about 1.3 kB per decade; its 6.0 kB lies between the hex20 slope (about 5.75 kB at 10M DOF) and its own (about 6.6 kB). Check it with the first 10M setup (the deferred GPU check runs it on the host).

### PR 13c decisions without user input

- **Option dicts, not flat keywords:** `operator_options`, `precond_options`, `sigma_options` keep the `solve_modes` signature stable as builders gain options, and avoid the clash between the operator's `chunk` (elements per scan step) and the solver's `chunk` (modes per locking chunk).
- **Options kept internal:** `lobpcg`'s `R`, `precond` and (PR 18b) `polish` (set by `solve_modes`; the polish tuned through `polish_options`); `amg`'s `nonsingular` (a proof, derived from the rigid-mode count) and `aggregates` / `return_aggregates` (state: compose the building blocks); `gmg`'s and (PR 18c) `amg`'s `dtype` (the top-level `precond_dtype`; the pre-estimate builds stay fp64 for robustness); `pmg` (test scale, superseded by `amg`); `block_jacobi` / `chebyshev` alone (without a coarse level they gain nothing, PR 10). Passing a pre-built operator or preconditioner to `solve_modes` is not supported; revisit if a user needs it.
- **No default changes:** every default is the robust option measured in PRs 9–13 (table in `docs/eigensolvers.md#options`); smoother degree 2 is the fastest in a full solve (7.0 / 8.8 / 10.3 s at degree 2 / 3 / 4) and stays.
- **`amg_memory` at other `max_coarse`:** the coarse term is floored at the calibration value 1000 (an upper bound at 100–3000, loose above 1000: 4.3× at 3000); `AMG_STEP` stays calibrated at 1000, so above it the step term is not a bound (+1.3 blocks of temporaries at 4000).
- **Chebyshev loop terms:** degree > 2 adds `AMG_LOOP_K` = 120k DOF-equivalents to `AMG_STEP`'s $K$ (fixed loop buffers, +77k–103k measured) and `GMG_LOOP` = 1.2 / 0.7 fp64 blocks (fp64 / fp32 V-cycle) to `gmg_memory` (+1.07 / 0.60 measured), calibrated on boxes like the other constants. `GMG_STEP` re-fitted after the loop lowered the default fp64 steps (4.3 / 9.0, was 5.1 / 9.9); `GMG_WORK` kept (now 7–39% above the measured temporaries at $32^3$–$64^3$).

### PR 14a decisions without user input

- **PR 14 split:** 14a ships the counting estimator (`count`); 14b ships the $[0, f_{max}]$ driver, the budget logic and `verify`. The roadmap tests are tagged [14a]/[14b]; no criterion is dropped.
- **Shift $\sigma = \lambda_c$:** the spectral map $X = 2\lambda_c T - I$ puts the cut at $x = 0$ and needs no new operator; the preconditioner is built at $\lambda_c$ like any `solve_modes` shift.
- **Probes from quadrature factors:** $M_e = B_e B_e^T$ with $B_e = N^T \operatorname{diag}\sqrt{w \det J}$ (exact, no Cholesky); lumped mass uses $\sqrt{\operatorname{diag} M}$. Constrained DOFs carry diagonal 1 in $M_P$ and are projected out of every probe (the roadmap's scaled diagonal is not needed: $g$ vanishes there, so $y$ is the same).
- **Weyl exponent $a = 3/2$ fixed** in the tail-bias bound (3D); a 2D or 1D-like body (slender beam, thin plate) has a slower-growing count, so the bound stays conservative.
- **Degree search:** doubling then bisection on the exact $q(x_l)^2 \ge 0.95$ and tail-bias $\le 0.05$ tests; `MAX_DEGREE` = 100 000 (beyond it `count_degree` returns `(None, nan)` and `count` is not run: the estimate is NaN, `verifiable` False; 14b must then move the cut or solve more modes).
- **Tail-bias grid $64d$ instead of the plan's $8d$:** the trapezoid on $8d$ points was 2.5% off `quad` (second order); $64d$ gives 0.04% and costs 1–2 s for a degree search near `MAX_DEGREE`. The ripple guard stays on $8d$.
- **Jacobi default inner preconditioner** for `count`'s `precond=None` (robust, no setup); callers (PR 14b's `verify`) pass the fp64 AMG/GMG built at $\lambda_c$.
- **Inner tolerance rule $\tau_c = 0.01/(d\sqrt{n_{free}})$ kept although far too strict (needs your input):** the counting study measures $|\Delta s| \le \tau s$ independent of $d$ (20–400), so at the default $\tau_c$ the error is about $10^{-6}$, five orders below the 0.5 threshold. Loosening it (e.g. to $\tau = 10^{-4}$, error $\le 10^{-4}$ with $s \approx 1$) would save 1.8–3.9 PCG iterations per decade, about a third of the PCG iterations on the measured meshes, but the roadmap fixes $\tau_c \lesssim 0.01/(d\sqrt{n})$ and roadmap criteria are not relaxed without you. Default: keep (still kept in PR 14b; decide before PR 15).
- **Counting cost:** at 200 modes, counting at a typical cut ($g$ = 0.002–0.04, $d$ = 500–11 000) is predicted at 10²–10³ solve times (median 160–250×); only cuts with $g \gtrsim 0.6$ cost a few solves (1.2–3.6×). `verify=None` in 14b will therefore rarely enable counting; 14b should place cuts at the largest gap near the band edge.
- **Early stop and NaN:** a block whose PCG solve fails (stagnation or `maxit`) stops the recurrence; the estimate is NaN and `verifiable` is False, so 14b never trusts a partial count.
- **Detection test:** 100 sets of 50 probes from one 5000-probe count; each probe has its own `fold_in` key, so the sets are i.i.d. and equal 100 independent 50-probe counts (the roadmap's fixed list of 100 seeds), and results do not depend on the block size (default one block; 14b sizes it with `probe_block`).
- **Orthonormality check:** $W^T M W = I$ is checked by a streamed random sketch (3 vectors) at `ORTHO_TOL` (1e-6 fp64, 1e-4 fp32), not a full Gram matrix.
- **Memory constants:** `COUNT_STEP`, `GMG_COUNT`, `AMG_COUNT` are fitted on box meshes (hex20 $12^3$–$28^3$, voxel $16^3$–$64^3$); the hex20 $8^3$ case (one chunk holds the mesh) is outside the fit. PR 18e: `COUNT_STEP`'s unstructured part refitted to hex20 $44^3$ and `ssh8` $515^2$ (valid to 1.6M DOF; the 10M check is a deferred GPU check).
- **No thin-plate counting test:** AMG on hex elements is weak on flat elements (see "Thin plates and slender beams"); the slender hex20 cantilever (κ 2.2e9) covers the high-κ case. Counting on `ssh8` with the solid-shell AMG is covered since PR 18b (`tests/test_shell_solve.py::test_band`, κ ≈ $3\cdot10^6$).

### PR 14b decisions without user input

- **Basis growth:** past its capacity (default $4\cdot$`chunk`, or `n_modes`) the locked basis grows 1.5× (at least room for the next lock) at lock time: a transient of old + new arrays, and the LOBPCG step recompiles for the new shape. No `max_modes` cap; passing `n_modes` near the expected count avoids growth. *Superseded in PR 15d:* the growth is capped at $C_{max}$ from `memory_budget` and frees and refills from the host V (no old + new transient).
- **Band chunk default 40** (block 48, as the 2M-DOF budget measured in PR 11b) without `n_modes`; with `n_modes`, $\min(40, \lceil 1.25\,n\rceil + 2)$. *PR 15d:* the top of the chunk ladder for $f_1 = 0$; 40 for $f_1 > 0$.
- **The a-priori bound's factor $c = 2$** assumes a count step costs at least 7 PCG iterations at width $b$ and a LOBPCG iteration at most about 3 applies at width $m$. Measured slack: the real step is 4.7–6.4× the bound's step at $m = 48$ (7× in `test_calibration`), but only 1.3× on the 2.7k-DOF box at $m = 11$, where LOBPCG's fixed costs dominate; the margin is only 1.3× there, so on tiny solves the bound may exceed the real cost on another machine or mesh and skip a count that would have fit (never a wrong count).
- **Two seams `_bound` and `_calibrate`** instead of one `_predict`: the tests patch each separately (the bound to force a calibration, the calibration to skip the count). *PR 15d:* moved to counting.py with `_decide` (the rule for all three sites), and the tests patch `counting`.
- **An explicit `count_options["degree"]` bypasses `MAX_DEGREE`** (the user's choice; only the degree rule's $d$ is capped, and `count` returns NaN only on that path).
- **`verify=True` keeps the modes when the count does not fit** (budget below one probe): it returns them with `ran` False, the reason and a warning instead of raising, because the check runs after the solve and raising would discard it.
- **Probe budget = the solve's estimate − host modes − kept aggregates:** `solve_modes_memory(band=...)` at the final capacity, minus $8\,n\,k$ for the converged modes on the host (shared memory on the Mac) and the AMG aggregates; the λ_c multigrid is counted at fp64. *PR 15d:* kept on the "fits" path (`band=(0, f₂)`), so this count ignores `memory_budget` and can exceed a user budget below the GPU pool.
- **The final chunk converges modes beyond the band** (`n_beyond`): they are needed for $\lambda_\text{next}$ and are deflated in the count, but not returned.
- **Host `V` holds `n_modes` + chunk columns** in band mode (one more block than a plain solve); `lobpcg_memory(band=True)` adds one block. *Superseded in PR 15d:* V is preallocated at $C_{max}$ + k columns, zeroed (paged lazily), F-order.
- **Band peak test:** voxel $48^3$, `n_modes=10` (chunk 15, block 20, inside the memory model's 15–48 fit) measured 2.83 GB against the 2.62 GB estimate, 7% under (within the 20% criterion).
- **The final chunk's re-orthonormalization is defensive:** at test sizes $\max|W^\top M W - I|$ is the same with and without it (3.8e-15 vs 4.2e-15 fp64, 7.5e-9 vs 7.7e-9 fp32, 3 chunks); it guards against the drift PR 9b measured after about 20 chunks.
- **$\tau_c$:** the band count uses the roadmap's $\tau_c$; decided in PR 15c (below).
- **AMG build peak** (PR 13b item above): *PR 15d:* the `build` term also bounds the count-time rebuild with the aggregates held (test 11).

### PR 15a decisions without user input

- **PR 15 split into 15a / 15b / 15c** (the one-slice solver; the band-pass count and restarts; slicing, strategy choice and `band=(f1, f2)`; PR 15c split again into 15c, the slicing engine, and 15d, the strategy and API), every roadmap PR 15 test assigned to one part (`docs/plans/pr-15a-shift-invert.md`).
- **Ritz pairs by Rayleigh–Ritz on (K, M)** over the shift-invert Krylov basis, not from the inexact projected $T$ (whose residuals exceeded $10\tau\delta$ and grew with the basis); the roadmap's "final Rayleigh–Ritz" is the last step's, with true residuals recomputed on the compact V. No implicit restarts: 15c splits a full slice.
- **Per-pair thresholds** $t_i = \max(tol, 10\tau_\text{eff}\delta_i, \text{round-off floor})$ and $r_{acc} = \max t_i$ over the in-slice pairs and the neighbours (the roadmap's $r_{acc}$ plus LOBPCG's floor term and the neighbours, so never below the roadmap's value).
- **σ at the slice midpoint** (or given), moved once by 1% of the width when the first solve stagnates above $10^3\max(\tau, 10^{-10})$ (the plan said $10^3\tau$; the floor term keeps a τ below the fp64 MINRES floor from moving σ); $\tau_\text{eff} > 10^{-4}$ or a non-finite MINRES residual ends the slice as "inner solves failed".
- **τ_eff** = the running max of the stagnated or capped columns' final residuals; later inner solves capped at 10× the first step's iterations (the first step at the σ used).
- **`capacity` is required** (no default allocation); since PR 15d `solve_modes` passes the searched slice capacity. The basis also stops at the free space left by R and W (beyond it new columns are round-off noise).
- **`lam` holds the converged `inside` set**, which extends 100 $r_{acc}$ past each edge and over clusters straddling one (the plan's "in $[\lambda_{lo}, \lambda_{hi}]$" read with its own 100 r window); 15c's duplicate removal handles the overlap.
- **`si_memory`** counts the build on top of the full storage plus a host term (as `lobpcg_memory`), and takes the expected slice count `n_modes` for its end phase. *Superseded in PR 15d:* `n_modes` is the store's columns, in the host term.
- **Cost driver:** the depth of σ in the spectrum, not δ, sets the MINRES iterations (measured); PR 15d fitted $\alpha D(1 + D/D_0)$ on depth (hex20 5.95 / 691, voxel 4.31 / 814), as evidence for the memory-driven strategy.
- **Largest-size benchmark cut (user: "Run something more reasonable", 2026-10-13).** The plan's 1M and 2M τ = 1e-14 floor solves run near mode 6, not 100 (about 2 min each), and the 2M voxel slice sits near mode 6 too. The 1M hex20 slice near mode 100 was measured before the cut (converged, 9 steps of about 780 MINRES iterations, 1.7 h). `shift_invert_study.py --parts` runs one part at a time and every slice prints a progress line per step.

### PR 15c decisions without user input

- **Counting tolerance $\tau_c$ kept at the roadmap rule** $0.01/(d\sqrt{n_{free}})$ (user, 2026-10-14: "Just decide for yourself"). Loosening it to $10^{-4}$ would save about a third of the PCG iterations of a count (PR 14a's study) but leave counts at 100× or more the solve at typical gaps, so it would not change which counts run, while the strict rule keeps the error about $10^{-6}$, far below the 0.5 threshold. `count_options["tau"]` sets it per call; PR 15d's per-leaf calibration prices counts at the τ in use.
- **PR 15c split into 15c (slicing engine `si_band`) and 15d (cost model, memory budget, strategy, `verify=None`, `band=(f1, f2)`)**, every roadmap PR 15 test assigned ([15c] / [15d] tags in the roadmap).
- **No provisional interior edges from a cost model:** the measured slice cost (about 1.8 Lanczos columns per mode plus about 84 per slice, a slice spending 50–85 before it converges anything) makes the widest slice that fits the cheapest; slices come from full slices reduced to their span and remainders, the converged span kept as a leaf without a second run. `edges` is an internal option, used as given. *PR 15d:* reachable through `slice_options`; this rationale is cited by the memory-driven strategy.
- **Edges come from full slices:** the span si_slice's completeness rule verifies on the run's final Ritz values, cut at the largest gap $\ge 100\,r_{acc}$ within each half of its converged run (the roadmap's snapping rule), a binary split on a side without a cut; a flagged span is rerun (at most `RESTARTS` times, below). The cut is the largest gap within the half on its side, not the run's overall largest (the roadmap says "largest gap"): a cut deep in the run moves up to half the run into the remainder's W, lowering its capacity, while the outermost gap can be tiny and the count's degree is $\propto 1/g$.
- **A done side gives up its half's gaps** (code review round 20). With one side complete, the other side cuts only in its own half (for $k$ even, the middle gap included since round 19); a qualifying gap in the done side's half is unused, so such a slice is requeued whole (at most one more full run; "no progress" only if that run finds nothing new, a genuine capacity shortfall). Decision without user input: keep the halves (the remainder's capacity rationale) rather than fall back to all gaps. 15d and PR 18e: kept (at most one more full run; no cost model to revisit it).
- **si_slice counts deflated W modes as converged candidates** (a W mode can close a side), and deflation windows include the nearest stored pair beyond each edge. Consequence: a mode across a boundary is deflated by the next slice instead of found twice, so the roadmap's collapse test sees a merged window without a duplicate (the plan expected one); duplicate removal is tested on fakes.
- **Remainders run at their midpoint** (si_slice's default σ).
- **Leaves keep si_slice's widened inside sets** (margins, straddling chains) rather than only the pairs inside their interval; deflation windows and the merge handle the overlap, so `lam` can hold pairs just outside the band.
- **Deflation windows:** margins $\min(1000 r_w, 10^{-2})$, chains at $100 r_w$, $r_w = \max(r_s, \texttt{tol})$; `capacity` is the total basis per slice, deflation included.
- **MINRES does not stagnate before its best explicit residual drops below $\|b\|$** (a plateau, not a stall), fixed where it surfaced (PR 15c); an inner failure stops the band without a seed retry.
- **`edge_unreached` is not evidence of an edge outside the spectrum:** no fallback in 15c; 15d applies the $f_1 < \lambda_1$ rule up front; an edge in $(\lambda_\max, \hat\lambda_\max]$ raises the capacity error, which names both causes. *Partly addressed in PR 15d:* the μ₁ rule is done. PR 18e closed the $\lambda_{hi}$ bound: the cheap rigorous bound (the largest element $\lambda_\max(K_e, M_e)$) is typically 2–4× above $\lambda_\max$, too loose to close a side; late errors only, now with `err.result`.
- **The merge** applies the roadmap's 0.5 projected-norm rule per direction of a run's window subspace, then Rayleigh–Ritz; a failing window is widened once (by the neighbouring windows), then flagged (`edge_rr_failed`).
- **Counts reuse the preconditioner of their slice** (a span leaf: its parent's, before it is released; built at the slice's σ, not at $c_{hi}$; only PCG iterations depend on it).
- **A flagged or unverifiable run leaf is not split or rerun** beyond 15b's restarts.
- **V is M-orthogonal to residual level across runs** that do not deflate each other (code review round 1: $1.6\cdot10^{-9}$ on modes 5–40 of hex20 $6\times5\times4$, about $4\cdot10^{-9}$ on hex20 $8\times6\times5$ modes 5–120, against $\le 10^{-13}$ within windows); the bound $(\|x_j\|\|r_i\| + \|x_i\|\|r_j\|)/|\lambda_i - \lambda_j|$ is exact and tested. No final M-orthonormalization across runs: it needs another streamed pass over V and changes the residuals by the same order.
- **A slice whose $r_{acc}$ widens its deflation window is rerun** with the extra stored pairs deflated (code review round 1: a slice with loose inner solves found a stored triple again, and the next slice raised on its non-orthonormal W). Rare (raised $\tau_\text{eff}$ only); the discarded run's count, and any restarts it triggered, are paid for and dropped too (code review round 18; PR 18e: kept, as it is rare, rarer with the per-slice τ, and the discarded count is paid once). The alternative, deduplicating at insertion, leaves kept vectors only residual-orthogonal to the stored ones near them.
- **Slow tests beyond the plan's fast budget:** the block-2 capacity split (60 s) and the capacity-72 raise (43–97 s, load-dependent) are marked slow; the fast tier keeps the block-6 split, the fakes and the unit tests of `_split`, `_window` and the merge rules.
- **Cross-run orthogonality inside a deflation window** (code review round 2): a window can hold stored pairs of two runs that did not deflate each other (the margin grows when $r_s$ rises between a parent and its remainder); their inner product is residual-level, $\lesssim 2r/g$ ($r$ the pairs' λ-scaled residuals, $g$ their relative gap), and at the default tol $10^{-6}$ and gaps near $10^{-2}$ it could in principle exceed si_slice's W check ($10^{-6}$ by sketch), which would raise. Not seen in any run (actual residuals sit well below tol); revisit if a slice raises on its W.
- **A flagged span is rerun at most `RESTARTS` = 2 times in its lineage** (its remainders inherit the number of reruns), then kept as a flagged leaf (the band's count flagged, not passed), as si_checked's restarts (code review round 14: a span flagged on every rerun looped to `max_slices`). A rerun starts with no side complete; remainders keep their parent's complete edges, so an interval never counts a side twice (code review round 14: an interval completing its sides in turn looped). Progress is checked only for a slice requeued whole; a cut shrinks the interval and both sides complete finish it (code review round 15: a remainder of a remainder closing both sides with nothing new raised "no progress").

### PR 15d decisions without user input

- **Memory-driven strategy, no time-based crossover.** LOBPCG to $D + N$ is cheaper than any slice at every measured $D$ and $N$ (hex20 $16^3$, AMG, to depth 1005: 33× at depth 6, 1260× at 1005; `benchmarks/results/strategy_study.json`). ### Bimodal LOBPCG peak on the 791k plate (PR 18c → PR 18d; user decision 2026-10-19)

The cold `solve_modes` on the flat $362^2$ plate (791k DOF, 20 modes, σ given, synchronous CPU dispatch, fresh processes) peaks in two regimes on identical code: fp32 AMG 4.75 GB (three runs) or 6.9–7.1 GB, fp64 6.29 GB or 7.0–9.4 GB (the high-regime lower ends are the recorded `amg_fp32_study.json` and 18b runs, 6.88 / 6.99 GB). The low regime lies within the model (`solve_modes_memory` 5.52 / 6.71 GB, 1.16× / 1.07× the peak) and matches `memory_analysis`; the high one adds 2–3 GB inside LOBPCG's iterations. Which regime a run lands in depends on process-level timing: a Python thread calling `proc_pid_rusage` every 5 ms gave the low regime every time, a sleep-only thread or none the high one; the GC threshold (50, or disabled) and synchronous dispatch do not change it. It is not specific to fp32 (fp64 reached 9.4 GB, 40% above its estimate, which is then 29% below the peak) and resembles the GMG intermittent extra peak (PR 11b, above); the 1M hex20 acceptance shows it on an idle machine (fp64 9.5–9.9 GB at the PR 13b commit itself, recorded 7.46 GB), not with three peak tests running at once. Decision (user): the estimators stay the `memory_analysis` envelope; `test_shell_791k_peak32` checks $0.75\,\text{peak} \le$ estimate $\le 1.2\,\text{peak}$, which covers both regimes; the fp64 tests keep their ±20% (they usually pass under `scripts/test.sh`'s three-at-a-time peak stage; the first PR 18c merge-gate run failed `test_mac_acceptance`, warm peaks 9.76 / 7.23 GB against 7.45 GB, and `test_mac_count_peak`, degree 2 / 8 peaks 6.24 / 5.44 GB, on code paths 18c does not change; the peak stage re-run passed all 28). The root cause (XLA CPU runtime or allocator timing) was a PR 18d item; it affects every peak test. PR 18d ([performance](performance.md#bimodal-lobpcg-peak), `bimodal_probe.json`): the extra is resident (`ru_maxrss` agrees); neither XLA flag tried (`--xla_cpu_multi_thread_eigen=false`, `--xla_cpu_enable_concurrency_optimized_scheduler`) moves it (10 of 10 flagged runs high, and 5 of 5 baseline runs); the process's own `proc_pid_rusage` call between LOBPCG steps moves every run to the low regime (8 of 8, as PR 18c's 5 ms polling thread did), `jax.live_arrays()` or a full-output sync does not, a parent's sampling partly. The cause is not found within the time box; nothing is adopted (a per-step syscall would be a macOS workaround for an unexplained effect), the estimators and 18c's gates stay, and the question moves to the GPU check.

### MINRES plateaus below $\|b\|$ (PR 15c)

PR 15c's stagnation rule skips plateaus only while the best explicit residual is $\ge \|b\|$. A plateau below $\|b\|$ in the 2-norm (another norm ratio, a better preconditioner) would still stop the slice as "inner solves failed", and the band with it. Not measured so far. A later fallback: continue a stagnated column once from its best iterate. While a column plateaus above $\|b\|$, `minres` returns its best *checked* iterate, which can be worse than $x = 0$ ($1.22\,\|b\|$ in the test case); a capped column counts as bad for si_slice's σ-move and τ_eff rules either way. Revisit if a band stops with "inner solves failed".

### Slice efficiency (PR 15c → PR 18e → PR 18f)

Code review rounds 1 (hex20 $8\times6\times5$, AMG; scratch measurements), 8 and 18 (rounds 7, 9 and 10: Resolved, "Late capacity errors and slice costs"):

- **Verification slices.** 11 of the 37 slices of modes 5–200 at capacity 96 (Resolved: "A capacity error discards the band") found at most one new mode: their pairs were already stored by the parent, and they ran 10–15 steps to close their edges. MINRES is about 92% of slice time. 15d prices no slice choice (the strategy is memory-driven); closing such remainders more cheaply → PR 18f.
- **A top remainder holding only known modes can raise.** Its upper neighbour must still converge at σ = its midpoint. Seen on the cube band [6.855, 39.384] at capacity 96 ("no progress" after all band modes were found); again in code review round 8 (cube from 0 to 28.008, capacity 96, tol $10^{-8}$: all 32 modes stored, the remainder [21.847, 28.008] leaves the neighbour 29.43 at residual $1.08\cdot10^{-7}$ against its threshold $1.53\cdot10^{-8}$). The capacity message then names a gap on either side and "lo may be below lambda_1" (also printed for lo = 0) although only the neighbour beyond an edge is missing. *PR 15d:* the message is worded per case; the cost → PR 18f (PR 18e kept "halving before a capacity error"). Option: σ toward an edge whose neighbour is missing.
- **A slice with new pairs but neither side complete nor cut is requeued whole** (code review round 18: it raised "neither side done or cut" although it had found a pair, when the converged run nearest σ reached no edge while another run did). Decision without user input: requeue (robustness over the error's cost). Each requeue is a full slice that may add as little as one pair, so a lineage can pay up to one run per mode in $[a, b]$ (capped by `max_slices`) before it closes a side or raises "no progress"; typically one more full-capacity run (no span count), since the deflated chain joins the next run and any converged neighbour inside gives a cut. PR 18e: the requeue reuses the preconditioner (same σ); moving σ toward the run's unconverged neighbour → PR 18f.

### Shift-invert cost near mode 1000 (PR 15a → PR 18e → PR 18f)

With σ near mode 1000 (hex20 $16^3$, AMG), a mid-gap MINRES solve takes about 14k iterations (4970–5260 at depth 500); at 1M DOF on the Mac that is about 4 h per slice step. Of two runs at the same settings one had a column stagnate (last iterate $6.8 \times 10^{-3}$) and one converged ($9.8 \times 10^{-9}$), the difference most likely from round-off (the recompiled loop) (the best-iterate fix leaves the iterates and exits unchanged): at that depth the inner solve is marginal, and σ near an eigenvalue stagnated there too. So the limit is both cost and possible failure. This matters for the 10M GPU goal, where LOBPCG's basis runs out near modes 700–1000 and the roadmap sends those bands to shift-invert. *PR 15d:* warned (`stagnation_risk` and the warning after a capacity stop at ≥ 1000 locked modes), not priced; remedies → PR 18f. Options if it is too slow: deflate the converged modes below σ from MINRES (PR 18e's probe: 1500 → 140 iterations at depth 197, 4960 → 220 at 503, all modes below σ deflated; PR 18f), a stronger (higher-degree or σ-aware) multigrid, or a memory-lean LOBPCG fallback. May need the user's input if no option meets the roadmap's 10M band criteria.

### σ-move retries (PR 15a → PR 18e → PR 18f)

A slice whose first solve stagnates moves σ once and repeats the solve; when the stagnation is depth-driven, the retry costs a second full solve before the slice stops. 15d's strategy places no slices by cost (memory-driven; the stagnation warning instead); a retry policy → PR 18f.

### Band-pass count and slice restarts (PR 15b, decisions without user input)

- **Tail model fitted to the band.** No mode index is known inside a band, so the Weyl count $N \propto \lambda^{3/2}$ is fitted to the in-band modes and the nearest pair on each side, with $N(0) = 0$ (rigid modes deflated). A band with few modes gives a rough fit; the 3/2 exponent stays the upper bound, and the tail binds only with very many modes below.
- **Only the nearest out-of-band pair per side is deflated.** Further modes add at most $q^2 \approx 7\cdot10^{-4}$ (at $g/2$ past an edge), inside the 0.05 bias. Adjacent slices' modes are not all deflated (memory and transfer cost).
- **σ = $c_{hi}$, the upper counting edge.** One recurrence; the lower edge costs $(1 + a)/(2\sqrt a)$ more degree (2× at $a = 0.07$). A σ between the edges would balance the edges. 15c's slices are as wide as memory allows, so wide leaves pay the lower edge's factor; it is recorded per leaf (`degree`, `gap`). PR 18e: closed (`verify=None` prices each count per leaf).
- **The slice's preconditioner is reused for the count** (built at the slice's σ, SPD): no second multigrid in memory; it costs PCG iterations when σ is far from $c_{hi}$ (wide slices). PR 15c: counts reuse the slice's preconditioner (PR 15c decisions above).
- **Restarts** deflate the found in-band modes, use fresh seeds for the start block and the probes, and stay within the first run's device memory (capacity reduced); at most 2. Unverifiable counts and unconverged slices are not restarted: a rerun would not change them. PR 15c: a flagged slice is not split (PR 15c decisions above).
- **The planted-mode test** scales the mass by $s$ to move one eigenvalue into a gap, instead of editing a mesh.
- **Progress** comes from a per-step `jax.debug.callback` (scalars only, rate-limited on the host); the verbose loop is a separate compile.
- **Slow tests.** The band expected-value tests (missing, free) and detection tests (missing, complete) take 21–111 s and are marked slow, above the plan's fast budget; they run in the full suite.

- **Late capacity errors and slice costs (PR 15c → PR 18e).** Resolved or closed in PR 18e; the remaining slice-efficiency items are open above (→ PR 18f).
  - **A capacity error discards the band.** Modes 5–200 at capacity 96 ran 37 slices (about 25 min) and then raised at a remainder. At 1M DOF a slice step can take hours. Remainders run at capacity 72 there because a 2-mode window costs a whole projector chunk (24 columns). Options for 15d: size the capacity with the window's chunk included; return the pairs found with `converged` False and the reason, as an inner failure does; or attach them to the error. *PR 15d:* the window is reserved in the capacity search ($2c$ device, $2(C - 2b)$ host); returning the pairs: PR 18e (`err.result`, with `complete_to`).
  - **Halving before a capacity error (code review round 16, fakes).** Since round 15 a full slice with a cut and no new pair splits instead of raising (raising there gave false capacity errors), so a hopeless remainder (an edge above the spectrum, a top remainder of known modes) pays at most about $\log_2 S + 1$ further runs, each with a span count, for $S$ stored modes above its last complete edge before its error (simulated with the real `_split`, code review round 20, uniform spacing: 3 runs in all at $S = 4$, 6 at $S = 32$; since round 19 the last $k = 2$ step with its lower side complete cuts, one more run and span count, instead of raising at once, which removed a false error in the non-hopeless case; such a cut on a gap near $100\,r$ can give an expensive count, see "Span counts at small capacities"); if the lower side is also cut each time, up to $O(S)$ runs (capped by `max_slices`). Decision without user input: keep the split (robustness over the error's cost). PR 18e: closed, the split kept; the up-front $\hat\lambda_\max$ bound closed (too loose), and the pairs found are returned with the error (`err.result`). 15d's `verify=None` skips the span counts of such halvings by their predicted cost.
  - **Per-step residuals of inside candidates (code review round 7, not measured).** `_slice_check` recomputes the explicit residuals of every inside candidate each step ($|\text{inside}|$ K products plus $2nk\,|\text{inside}|$ flops). Full slices are the normal case in 15c, so a shallow, wide slice may spend about as much there as in MINRES; `info["time"]["ritz"]` records it. PR 18e: closed (no measured case where it matters).
  - **`verify=False` still does input work.** A span count with `verify=False` still gathers its pairs and runs the counts' W check before returning the predicted degree (a host copy of up to one slice's V plus two streamed passes); PR 18e: the gather is lazy (a predicted skip and `verify=False` copy nothing); the W check runs only in the count itself (since PR 15d code review round 1).

PR 18e: closed (merges are rare, the merge peak is not budgeted, and the rewrite costs one K apply per column; revisit if a measured case shows the merge sets the process peak).
